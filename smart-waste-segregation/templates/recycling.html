How Recycling Works

Step-by-step guide to the recycling process for different materials

Paper Recycling
1
Collection & Sorting

Paper collected and sorted by type. Contaminated paper removed.

2
Pulping

Paper mixed with water and chemicals to break into fibers, creating pulp.

3
Cleaning & De-inking

Pulp screened to remove staples, tape, and ink. Uses 70% less energy.

4
Bleaching

Pulp bleached for whiteness and refined for fiber bonding.

5
Paper Making

Pulp sprayed on mesh, pressed and dried to form new paper.

6
Distribution

New paper becomes notebooks, boxes, or packaging.

Fact: Recycling 1 tonne of paper saves 17 trees and 26,500 liters of water.
Plastic Recycling
1
Collection & Sorting

Plastic sorted by type (PET, HDPE, PVC). Each has different recycling paths.

2
Shredding & Washing

Plastic shredded into flakes and washed to remove labels and residue.

3
Melt Filtration

Flakes melted and filtered. Formed into pellets called nurdles.

4
Pelletizing

Molten plastic formed into strands, cooled, and cut into uniform pellets.

5
Manufacturing

Pellets molded into bottles, containers, fibers, park benches.

6
New Products

Recycled plastic becomes carpet, clothing, furniture, new bottles.

Important: Only 9% of all plastic ever made has been recycled.
Glass Recycling
1
Collection

Glass bottles collected. Empty but not perfectly clean needed.

2
Sorting by Color

Sorted: clear, green, brown. Different colors recycled separately.

3
Crushing to Cullet

Glass crushed into small pieces. Metal rings removed by magnets.

4
Melting

Cullet melted at 1500°C. Uses 30% less energy than raw materials.

5
Forming

Molten glass blown into new bottles. Slowly cooled to prevent cracking.

6
Quality Check

Bottles inspected, filled, and sent to stores. Can recycle in 30 days!

Fact: Recycling one glass jar saves enough energy to power a light bulb for 4 hours.
Metal (Aluminum & Steel) Recycling
1
Collection & Sorting

Aluminum and steel separated using magnets.

2
Shredding

Metal shredded to increase surface area for melting.

3
Melting

Aluminum melts at 660°C, steel at 1370°C. Uses 95% less energy!

4
Purification

Molten metal purified to remove impurities and meet quality standards.

5
Casting

Metal cast into ingots or billets for manufacturing.

6
New Products

Recycled aluminum becomes new cans in 60 days! Steel becomes cars.

Fact: Recycling one aluminum can saves enough energy to run your TV for 3 hours.
E-Waste Recycling
1
Collection & Dismantling

E-waste manually dismantled. Hazardous components removed first.

2
Data Destruction

All data devices securely wiped or physically destroyed.

3
Shredding

Components shredded. Magnets separate metals, plastics, ceramics.

4
Metal Recovery

Gold, silver, platinum extracted. Circuit boards have 40-800x more gold than ore!

5
Hazardous Treatment

Lead, mercury safely contained and disposed per regulations.

6
Material Recovery

Copper, aluminum, steel become raw materials for new products.

Warning: Never burn or dump e-waste! Always use authorized recyclers.
Home Composting
1
Collect Kitchen Waste

Gather vegetable peels, fruit scraps, tea leaves. Avoid meat, dairy.

2
Layer with Dry Waste

Mix green waste with brown (leaves, cardboard) in 1:2 ratio.

3
Maintain Moisture

Keep moist like wrung-out sponge. Too dry slows, too wet smells.

4
Turn Regularly

Mix every 2-3 weeks for oxygen. Speeds decomposition.

5
Wait 2-3 Months

Temperature rises as microbes work. Pile darkens and reduces.

6
Use in Garden

Finished compost is dark, crumbly, smells like forest soil!

Benefits: Reduces landfill waste, enriches soil, eliminates chemical fertilizers.

Waste Management Hierarchy

1. Prevention - Don't create waste
2. Reduction - Use less, waste less
3. Reuse - Use again before discarding
4. Recycling - Convert to new products
5. Disposal - Landfill (last resort)
