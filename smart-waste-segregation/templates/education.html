Waste Management & Conservation

Learn how proper waste management helps conserve natural resources

Solid Waste Management Rules 2016
Who is Responsible?
  • Households: Must segregate waste into 3 bins
  • Local Bodies: Collection, transportation, processing
  • Commercial: Must ensure proper waste management
  • Waste Pickers: Integrated into formal system
Key Features
  • Segregation: 3 categories: wet, dry, hazardous
  • Producer Responsibility: Companies take back packaging
  • Penalties: Fines for improper disposal
  • Composting: Mandatory for housing societies

Types of Waste

Organic / Wet Waste

Biodegradable waste from kitchens and gardens.

Examples:
  • Vegetable peels, fruit scraps, food leftovers
  • Tea leaves, coffee grounds, egg shells
  • Garden waste, leaves, flowers
Handle: Compost at home or put in green bin.
Recyclable / Dry Waste

Non-biodegradable waste that can be recycled.

Examples:
  • Paper, cardboard, notebooks
  • Plastic bottles, containers, bags
  • Glass bottles, metal cans
Handle: Clean and put in blue bin.
Hazardous Waste

Waste containing toxic substances.

Examples:
  • Batteries, e-waste, phones
  • Medicines, syringes, medical waste
  • Paint, chemicals, CFL bulbs
Handle: NEVER mix with other waste. Take to collection points.
Domestic Hazardous

Common hazardous items in households.

Examples:
  • Used cooking oil, motor oil
  • Empty detergent, shampoo bottles
  • Mosquito coils, insect repellents
Handle: Rinse containers. Dispose chemicals at facilities.
Problems
  • Landfills produce methane, 25x more potent than CO2
  • Microplastics found in drinking water and human blood
  • Open burning releases toxic chemicals
  • 1 million seabirds die yearly from plastic
Solutions
  • Segregate waste at source
  • Composting reduces wet waste by 80%
  • Recycling 1 can saves energy for 3 hours of TV
  • E-waste recycling recovers gold, silver, copper
