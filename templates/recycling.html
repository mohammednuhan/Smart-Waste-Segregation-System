Recycling

How Recycling Works.
Waste → New Life.

Have you ever wondered what happens to a bottle after you put it in the blue bin? Follow the full journey of these six materials.

The Journey

From Your Bin to a New Product

Each material has its own recycling path. Here's exactly how six everyday materials are reborn.

Paper Recycling
1
Collection & Sorting

Paper collected and sorted by type. Contaminated paper is removed.

2
Pulping

Paper is mixed with water and chemicals to break into fibres.

3
Cleaning & De-inking

Pulp is screened to remove ink, staples and tape — using 70% less energy.

4
New Paper

Clean fibres are pressed and dried into fresh notebooks, boxes and packaging.

Fact: One tonne of recycled paper saves 17 trees and 26,500 litres of water.
Plastic Recycling
1
Collection & Sorting

Plastic sorted by type — PET, HDPE, PVC — each has its own path.

2
Shredding & Washing

Plastic is shredded into flakes and washed to remove labels and residue.

3
Melt & Pelletize

Flakes are melted, filtered and formed into uniform plastic pellets.

4
New Products

Pellets become bottles, clothing, carpet and even park benches.

Heads up: Only about 9% of all plastic ever made has been recycled.
Glass Recycling
1
Collection

Bottles and jars collected — empty but not necessarily spotless.

2
Sorting by Colour

Sorted into clear, green and brown streams, each recycled separately.

3
Crushing to Cullet

Glass crushed into small pieces; metal rings removed by magnets.

4
Re-melting

Cullet melted at ~1500°C and blown into brand-new bottles.

Fact: One recycled jar powers a light bulb for 4 hours; glass is infinitely recyclable.
Metal (Aluminium & Steel)
1
Collection & Sorting

Aluminium and steel separated using powerful magnets.

2
Shredding

Metal shredded to increase surface area for efficient melting.

3
Melting

Aluminium melts at 660°C, steel at 1370°C — using 95% less energy!

4
New Products

Recycled aluminium becomes new cans in ~60 days. Steel becomes cars.

Fact: One recycled can saves enough energy to run a TV for 3 hours.
E-Waste Recycling
1
Collection & Dismantling

E-waste manually dismantled; hazardous components removed first.

2
Data Destruction

All storage devices securely wiped or physically destroyed.

3
Metal Recovery

Gold, silver and copper extracted — circuit boards hold up to 800x more gold than ore.

4
Sustainable Treatment

Lead and mercury safely contained; remaining materials become raw resources.

Warning: Never burn or dump e-waste — always use authorised recyclers.
Home Composting
1
Collect Kitchen Waste

Peels, fruit scraps, tea leaves. Avoid meat and dairy for home bins.

2
Layer with Dry Waste

Mix greens with browns (leaves, cardboard) in a 1:2 ratio.

3
Maintain & Turn

Keep it moist and turn every 2–3 weeks to supply oxygen.

4
Use in Your Garden

In 2–3 months you get dark, crumbly compost — free natural fertiliser.

Benefit: Cuts household waste by up to 50% and enriches your soil.

Waste Management Hierarchy

The best waste is the waste that is never created. Here's what matters most, in order.

  • 1 · Prevention Don't create waste
  • 2 · Reduction Use less, waste less
  • 3 · Reuse Use again before discarding
  • 4 · Recycling Convert to new products
  • 5 · Disposal Landfill — last resort only
