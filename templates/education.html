Policy

Solid Waste Management Rules 2016

India's national framework for responsible waste handling — and why segregation starts at home.

Who Is Responsible?
  • Households — must segregate waste into wet, dry and hazardous bins.
  • Local Bodies — responsible for collection, transport and processing.
  • Commercial Establishments — must manage their waste responsibly.
  • Waste Pickers — integrated into the formal waste system.
Key Features
  • Source Segregation — wet, dry and hazardous separated at the home.
  • Producer Responsibility — companies take back their packaging.
  • Penalties — fines for improper disposal of waste.
  • Mandatory Composting — for bulk waste generators and societies.
Types of Waste

Knowing the Difference Changes Everything

Every waste type has a different lifecycle — and a different correct destination.

Organic / Wet

Biodegradable waste from kitchens and gardens. The perfect raw material for compost.

Compost at home or use the Green bin.
Recyclable / Dry

Paper, plastic, glass and metals. Non-biodegradable, but highly valuable when clean.

Clean it and use the Blue bin.
Hazardous

Batteries, e-waste, medicines and chemicals. Toxic to both people and the planet.

Never mix — use special collection points.
Domestic Hazardous

Everyday chemicals — cooking oil, detergents, repellents. Common yet still dangerous.

Rinse containers & use safe disposal.
Problems & Solutions

The Price of Careless Disposal

Waste doesn't disappear when it leaves your home — it goes somewhere. The question is whether that's a landfill or a new life.

The Problems
  • Landfills release methane — 25x more potent than CO₂.
  • Microplastics found in drinking water & human blood.
  • Open burning releases toxic chemicals into the air.
  • 1 million seabirds die from plastic every year.
The Solutions
  • Segregate at source — it costs nothing and solves most of the problem.
  • Composting reduces wet waste by up to 80%.
  • Recycling one can powers a TV for 3 hours.
  • E-waste recycling recovers gold, silver & copper.
Take Action

What Can You Do Today?

Real change starts with individual habits. Start with these four simple actions.

Segregate

Keep separate bins for wet, dry and hazardous waste at home.

Compost

Start a simple compost pit to convert kitchen waste into garden gold.

Spread Awareness

Teach family and friends the right way to sort their waste.

Participate

Join local clean-up drives and community composting groups.
